\small\textsc{Abstract.}
This closing report records the state of the attempt to rule out Rupert's
property for the parabigyrate rhombicosidodecahedron (Johnson solid J73).
When production stopped, the run had recorded 734 of its 2,057 staged
roots as closed. The other 1,323 remained open. In a separate pilot,
local replay verified closures for 22 roots still listed as open in
production. The report
describes the difficulty of certifying exclusion near coincident
projections and summarizes the local methods tested there. The full
exclusion cover remains incomplete, and the Rupert question for J73
remains open.
\end{quote}

\section*{What a complete proof would require}

A solid has Rupert's property if a congruent copy can pass through a
straight tunnel in it with positive clearance. For a convex solid, the
equivalent planar test uses orthographic projections of two independently
oriented copies onto a common plane. One projection must fit strictly
inside the other after translation, as described by
\href{https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3831}{Steininger and Yurkevich}.
Central symmetry allows the shadows of J73 to be centered for this
test. The \interimpaper{} gives the exact geometry, the symmetry
reduction and the local exclusion theorems.

The computation starts with a finite collection of closed boxes, called
\emph{roots}, that cover the reduced configuration space. A root can be
excluded by a rule valid on the entire box or by verified rules on
subboxes whose union covers it. A complete proof of exclusion needs a
complete cover and exact replay of the retained certificate chain from
every original root. The results below document progress toward that
criterion.

\section*{Exclusion near coincident projections}

For fixed orientations, let $\lambda$ be the supremum of the scale
factors for which the inner projection fits strictly inside the outer
projection. When the projections coincide, $\lambda$ equals one and
their boundaries coincide.
Coincidences occur at identical orientations, at orientations related
by symmetries of J73, and at the distinct orientations described in
the \interimpaper. Splitting can help away from coincidence. A box
still containing such a pose requires a local argument that allows
a zero gap.

\section*{Methods retained}

\textbf{Weighted support (WS).} Choose pairs of outer vertices whose
projected chord lies on a supporting line throughout the box. Form a
nonnegative weighted sum of the signed gaps before bounding its
derivatives, so that cancellations between the gaps are kept. A certified positive lower bound for that
combination excludes strict containment throughout the box.

\textbf{Corner mixed barrier and tubes.} Near a corner where
equal-shadow families meet, the corner estimate combines support
witnesses to give a nonnegative gap, with equality along the coincidence
branches. The tube estimates likewise use transverse bounds in local
coordinates to cover neighborhoods of known coincidence families.
Applying either estimate requires a verified enclosure of the entire
box in the region where the estimate holds.

In a sample of 48 roots taken from copied pending frontiers, the WS
and corner tests covered 89.86\% of pending boxes without extra
splitting. Allowing extra splits to depth~4 covered every pending box
in 16 of the 48 roots, and depth~10 raised that count to 17. A later
sweep tested every pending box in the 1,271 roots that had completed
stage two and were still open when the input was collected. Without
further subdivision, the version~3 corner and weighted-support checks
covered 832,921 of 977,075 boxes (85.25\%). They covered every pending
box in 285 roots. The sweep did not change the production count.
Covering the pending boxes checks only the unfinished part of a staged
root. A proof for an original grid root must also verify the earlier
certificates and each link between stages.

\textbf{Stage budgets and local replay.} Each stage revisits the
remaining roots with a larger cumulative time budget.
Remote search can propose certificates. Local exact replay decides
whether the returned evidence certifies the assigned region.

\section*{Closure and resumption}

The author stopped the project to pursue other work, expecting further
searches to take weeks to months. The time needed for a complete proof
is unknown. The calculation
state and replay tools were retained. To resume, run a full preflight
check against the retained state, continue the unresolved search and
bind any adopted remote certificates to the production proof chain.
The remaining requirement is a complete and independently checked
cover of the original roots.
